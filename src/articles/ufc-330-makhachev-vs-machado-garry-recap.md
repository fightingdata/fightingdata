UFC 330 landed at Xfinity Mobile Arena in Philadelphia, PA with the promotion's first trip back to the city since 2019. The building responded with 19,238 people and a $6.7 million gate. Two champions had their titles on the line against rising challengers, and a decade-long record was on the line in the main event. Islam Makhachev won his 17th straight, passing Anderson Silva for the longest win streak in UFC history. Edson Barboza laid his gloves in the center of the Octagon after competing in 32 UFC fights. We also saw a rare "Scottish Twister" that has only been pulled off one other time in UFC history.

Twelve fights, eight finishes, and two hours and twenty minutes of total fight time. The prelims and main card could have had more exciting matchups — it was the two title fights that drew people into watching this card.

## Prelims

#### Fight 1: Jeremiah Wells def. Myktybek Orolbai — Technical Submission (Guillotine Choke), Round 3 (1:24)

The card opened with a Philadelphia fighter against a -1200 favorite, and for two rounds it went exactly the way the odds said it would.

[[Jeremiah Wells]] actually started well. He cracked [[Myktybek Orolbai]] early and was throwing some hard shots in the opening exchanges, landing 11 of 22 in the first round. Then Orolbai caught a body kick, put him on the mat, and the fight disappeared. Orolbai went 16 of 17 on the ground in that round, took the back, and started landing shots on Wells. He finished the first two rounds with 4 of 6 takedowns and 6:20 of control time to Wells' 23 seconds. Wells did almost nothing in the second round. Down two rounds, Wells had no answer for the grappling.

Then Wells landed a right hand square on the jaw. Orolbai shot in to recover, put his head in exactly the wrong place, and Wells locked the guillotine. Orolbai went to sleep at 1:24 of the third.

Wells was +2800 to win by submission. He took his gloves off afterward and said the UFC decides his future — he wants a top 15 opponent, and if he can't get one, he's done. He talked about working in that building when he was younger. He talked about his son graduating from Air Force boot camp next week. It was a hell of a way to open a card.

#### Fight 2: Neil Magny def. Ramiz Brahimaj — TKO, Round 2 (3:20)

Neil Magny does this. He has been doing it for thirteen years and people still get surprised.

[[Ramiz Brahimaj]] had the physical edge and used it immediately. He put [[Neil Magny]] on his back, went to full mount, flattened him out, and controlled 4:18 of the opening round while Magny logged zero seconds of control. There was an armbar look that came and went. It was ugly, low-damage, and entirely one-sided. Magny landed 7 significant strikes in that round.

The thing about grinding Neil Magny is that it costs you more than it costs him. Brahimaj went 1 for 3 on takedowns for the fight, and every fence clinch he initiated took something out of the tank. Midway through the second, Magny swept, got up, got fence control, and started teeing off. He landed 17 of 27 in that round, 13 of 17 of it on the ground, and Brahimaj just knelt there and absorbed it until referee Jason Herzog stepped in at 3:20.

That's Magny's 25th UFC win, tying Charles Oliveira for the second-most in promotion history behind Jim Miller's 28. It was also his 38th UFC fight, the most any welterweight has ever had, and his 25 wins are the most in divisional history too. He's 3-1 in his last four with all three wins by finish. At 39, he is somehow in the best form he's had in years.

#### Fight 3: Lucas Fernando def. Rafael Tobias — TKO, Round 3 (1:10)

Two Brazilians at light heavyweight, one making his debut, and it was one of the cleanest debuts you'll see.

[[Lucas Fernando]] came out technical and never stopped. Jab, calf kick, front kick to the body, knee, right hand — he mixed everything and [[Rafael Tobias]] never figured out which one was coming. By the middle of the first round it was 22 to 4 on significant strikes. Tobias managed one moment of offense all night, catching a kick and dumping Fernando on his back, and Fernando pushed him off with his legs and got right back to work.

The second round was worse for Tobias. A massive jab sat him down, Fernando let him back up, and then just kept piecing him apart. The final numbers are lopsided in a way you rarely see across three rounds: Fernando 69 of 115 at 60%, Tobias 13 of 56 at 23%. Fernando went 14 of 15 on leg kicks and 18 of 20 to the body. Tobias went 0 for 8 in the third.

The finish was a knee to the body at distance. Tobias' body simply quit on him and he dropped. Keith Peterson called it at 1:10. Two knockdowns, a 60% clip, and a body attack that broke a man down over three rounds — that's a professional debut.

#### Fight 4: Tresean Gore def. Vicente Luque — Unanimous Decision (30-27, 30-27, 29-28)

A physically grueling fight on the card, and the one where a veteran's tank ran dry.

The first round was genuinely close. [[Vicente Luque]] outlanded [[Tresean Gore]] 26 to 22 working the jab and calf kicks, but Gore had the heavier hands and hurt Luque with a right hand midway through. You could argue it either way.

The second round is where it turned, and it turned in a way I didn't expect. Luque dropped Gore to his knees with a left hook and locked up a guillotine that looked like it might be the finish. He held it for what felt like a full minute. Gore rode it out, escaped, and reversed — and Luque had nothing left. He spent the rest of the round on his back eating elbows and defending an arm triangle. Gore went 24 of 34 at 70% in that round with 22 of 24 of his strikes coming on the ground.

Worth noting: neither man landed a single takedown all night. Gore was 0 for 2, Luque 0 for 1. All of that ground time came out of the guillotine sequence collapsing.

The third was Luque surviving. His body language was exhausted from the opening seconds and he barely threw for the first three minutes. Gore jabbed him up, took the round, and took the fight. Gore has now won two in a row and has real power in his hands. He also had some of his bottom teeth knocked out by a knee during the last minute of the third round. During the post-fight interview the broadcast showed the knocked out teeth.

#### Fight 5: Donte Johnson def. Eric McConico — KO, Round 1 (1:38)

Ninety-eight seconds, one punch, and one of the scariest finishes of the year.

[[Donte Johnson]] came out swinging and [[Eric McConico]] actually got the better of the opening exchange, landing a jab that hurt Johnson badly. That's the part people will forget. Johnson is gas-heavy and throws bombs, and for about a minute it looked like it might cost him.

Then he threw a straight left down the middle. It went through McConico's guard and landed flush, and McConico went limp before he hit the canvas. Johnson added a couple more that weren't necessary and referee Blake Grice waved it off at 1:38.

Johnson is 9-0 now with eight of his nine wins by stoppage and seven of those eight by knockout. He is an explosion with a fight record attached. McConico has now lost all five of his career fights by knockout.

#### Fight 6: Charles Johnson def. Eduardo Chapolin — Submission (Scottish Twister), Round 3 (1:36)

Have you ever heard of the "Scottish Twister"?

[[Eduardo Chapolin]] took this fight on days' notice after Jose Ochoa withdrew during fight week, at a 130-pound catchweight, for his UFC debut. He then went out and nearly won it. He chopped [[Charles Johnson]]'s lead leg all through the first round, stuffed every takedown Johnson attempted, and pushed the pace. He also lost a point in that round for a knee to a grounded opponent, which turned a 10-9 into a 9-9.

The second round was a scare. Chapolin cracked Johnson with a left hook that put him down — the only knockdown of the fight — and chased him around the cage looking to finish. He couldn't get it, and chasing cost him everything. By the last minute of the round his movement was gone.

Johnson looked like the fresher man in the third and started dictating. A front kick to the body hurt Chapolin, Chapolin shot, Johnson stuffed it and immediately took his back. As Chapolin tried to turn into him, Johnson locked up a Scottish twister. Tap at 1:36.

That is the fifth twister in UFC history and only the second Scottish twister, after Murtazali Magomedov earlier this year. The striking numbers were far closer than the fight felt — Johnson 66 of 142, Chapolin 55 of 131 — and Johnson went 0 for 5 on takedowns. He got it done with the one thing nobody was looking for. Afterward he dedicated the win to Allan Nascimento, the flyweight who passed away earlier this month.

#### Fight 7: Chidi Njokuani def. Joel Alvarez — Unanimous Decision (30-27, 30-27, 29-28)

The crowd booed this fight and I understand why. The numbers say it was a clinic.

[[Chidi Njokuani]] landed 155 significant strikes to [[Joel Alvarez]]'s 74, at 67% accuracy. That is an enormous output number for a fight that got booed for fifteen minutes. He went 48 of 53 on leg kicks — 91% — and 56 of 76 to the body. He landed more to the body than to the head. The gameplan was to take Alvarez's legs and midsection away and then coast on the scorecards, and it worked completely.

Alvarez, on late notice for Geoff Neal, never adjusted. He got his 1-2 off when he could get close, landed a nice right hook here and there, and by the third round he couldn't push off his lead leg. He needed to force something and never did. That's my issue with this fight — not that Njokuani was boring, but that Alvarez accepted every single thing that was being done to him.

Njokuani was a +260 underdog and snapped a two-fight skid. He got busier as it went, landing 46, 48, and 61 in the three rounds, with zero grappling from either man in fifteen minutes.

## Main Card

#### Fight 8: Esteban Ribovics def. Edson Barboza — TKO, Round 2 (1:32)

Edson Barboza's pre-fight walkout was emotional and everyone in the building knew what was coming.

[[Esteban Ribovics]] fought like a man who had no interest in a ceremony. He pressured from the first bell, landed 41 of 89 in the opening round to [[Edson Barboza]]'s 24 of 49, and went 14 of 18 in the clinch for the fight. Elbows, knees, a flying knee, combinations — the pace never let up. Barboza had his moments early with a strong calf kick and body work, but by the middle of the round his right eye was cut and by the end of it he had a gash split open across the top of his shin.

The second round lasted 92 seconds. Ribovics landed an uppercut, hunted Barboza down, and started throwing nasty elbows until referee Herb Dean stepped in. No knockdowns registered all fight — this was pure accumulation on a 40-year-old who had nothing left to answer with.

Barboza took his gloves off and laid them on the canvas in tears with his kids in the Octagon. Thirty-two UFC fights. He retires with the second-most knockouts by kick or knee in UFC history at six. Ribovics called him an idol and said that when you fight in this sport, you know this day comes for everyone.

#### Fight 9: Dustin Stoltzfus def. Mansur Abdul-Malik — Submission (Rear Naked Choke), Round 2 (4:25)

The biggest upset on the main card, and it came from the guy who was supposed to be outgunned everywhere.

[[Mansur Abdul-Malik]] is the better athlete. He's quicker, he's longer, and in open space he was landing the cleaner combinations. [[Dustin Stoltzfus]] decided none of that was going to matter. He stole the first round on grappling — 29 of 46 with 10 of 12 on the ground — including a slam where he genuinely got airborne before landing on top. He took the back, hunted a choke, and had Abdul-Malik scrambling for the last thirty seconds.

The second was chaos, both men trading knees and fists in scrambles, and Abdul-Malik actually outlanded him 22 to 14 in the round. Then Stoltzfus landed the right hand that stunned him. From there it was a straight line: guillotine attempt, hammerfists, back take, rear naked choke at 4:25.

These two used to train together. Stoltzfus finished with 43 of 70 at 61%, 3:02 of control, and a $100,000 Performance of the Night bonus. Abdul-Malik is 0-2 in his last two now, and the pattern is getting hard to ignore — the moment someone grabs him, the fight stops being his.

#### Fight 10: Jalin Turner def. Kaue Fernandes — KO, Round 1 (0:39)

Thirty-nine seconds. So fast, I couldn't even take notes on what happened.

[[Kaue Fernandes]] opened with a head kick attempt and a calf kick, then threw a spinning back fist. [[Jalin Turner]] met him coming out of the spin with a left hand that turned the lights off, followed him to the ground, and landed 8 of 10 ground strikes before Jason Herzog jumped in.

Turner finished 9 of 14 with a knockdown. That's the whole fight. He wants a number next to his name and called for a top 15 opponent, and he also took home Performance of the Night. When you spin in front of a fighter with that reach and that kind of timing, this is what happens.

#### Fight 11: Champion Mackenzie Dern def. #5 Gillian Robertson — Unanimous Decision (49-46, 49-46, 48-47) — Women's Strawweight Title

[[Mackenzie Dern]]'s first title defense, and she won it going away.

The opening round was as close to a shutout as a round gets: 19 to 3 on significant strikes, a takedown, 2:28 of control, and a rear naked choke locked up before the horn that was one angle adjustment away from ending the fight. Two of the three judges scored a 10-8 somewhere in this fight, and that's the round. [[Gillian Robertson]] did nothing. Her corner told her to wake the f*** up between rounds and they were right to.

Dern's striking is the underrated part of this. She was springing in behind the jab, landing calf kicks and body kicks, and she looked far more comfortable on the feet than the woman across from her. Then she'd level change whenever she wanted. She went 4 of 6 on takedowns and controlled 10:48 to Robertson's 6:47. The third round was her most active at 30 of 70.

Robertson's only real round was the fifth. She got a takedown, controlled 4:28, and landed 11 of 14 on the ground. But there was no power behind any of it — it looked like sparring — and Dern was content to defend off her back and ride it out to the belt. Dern said afterward her legs started to fade in the championship rounds and that she badly wanted the submission.

Her 12 wins in UFC strawweight competition are the second-most in divisional history behind Angela Hill's 13. The fight was low-action and the crowd let them know, but this was a dominant title defense by any measure.

#### Fight 12: Champion Islam Makhachev def. #1 Ian Machado Garry — Unanimous Decision (49-46, 49-46, 48-47) — Welterweight Title

Here's the number that will confuse people: [[Ian Machado Garry]] outlanded [[Islam Makhachev]] 29 to 22 in significant strikes over five rounds. He lost 49-46 on two cards.

That's because Makhachev landed 7 of 15 takedowns and controlled 12 minutes and 30 seconds to Garry's 34 seconds. This was a wrestling win, start to finish, and it was the closest thing to a struggle we've seen from him in a long time.

The first round was Makhachev getting a leg, taking the back, and grinding — 3:38 of control, knees to the body, constant weight, and Garry defending well enough to get back up repeatedly without ever solving it. The second round is where the champion looked like the champion. Garry initiated the clinch, and Makhachev landed a right hand followed by a left kick that caught Garry flush on the jaw and dropped him. Makhachev chased him down, took top position, worked for an arm triangle, and rode out the round. That's the only knockdown of the fight and his only real striking round — 11 of 20.

Then it flipped. Garry took the third clearly, 9 to 5, with Makhachev logging just 16 seconds of control and looking like he was fatiguing. High kicks, front kicks, inside kicks, a stuffed takedown. In the fourth Garry outlanded him 7 to 3 and went a perfect 5 for 5 to the body — but Makhachev hit two takedowns and controlled 3:05, and that's what the judges rewarded. The fifth was the same shape: Garry went 7 of 9 at 77% while Makhachev went 0 for 3, and Makhachev took him down twice and spent 3:12 on his back.

So Garry won the striking in three of five rounds and lost all three on control. That's the whole fight in one sentence, and it's why the 48-47 card exists.

Makhachev walked out with facial damage, which tells you something in itself. He also walked out with the longest win streak in UFC history at 17, passing Anderson Silva's 16 — a record that had stood for over thirteen years — and became just the fifth fighter to successfully defend titles in two weight classes, joining Daniel Cormier, Henry Cejudo, Amanda Nunes, and Jon Jones. He hasn't lost since October 2015. He recorded zero submission attempts in 12 and a half minutes of control, which is the one criticism you can level at the performance.

Garry was gracious in defeat. He said he has no complaints and no excuses, pointed at Justin Gaethje and Alexander Volkanovski still winning world titles at 38, and said nobody beats him in the mental game. I believe him. He was closer to this than most people expected and he's 28 years old. Run it back in eighteen months and I think it could be a different outcome.

In his post-fight press conference, Makhachev said he needs to fight before Ramadan begins in February 2027. He emphasized that he is 34 years old and wants to stay active before the religious fasting period.

## Night in Review

Two title defenses, eight finishes, and two hours and twenty minutes of total fight time. The prelims gave us a hometown comeback submission, a 39-year-old rewriting divisional records, a debut clinic, and the second Scottish twister in UFC history. The main card gave us a legend's retirement, a big time upset, a 39-second knockout, and a main event where one of the greatest records of all time fell.

**Biggest Risers:**
- Islam Makhachev — 17 straight, sole owner of the record, and a two-division defender
- Lucas Fernando — the most complete debut on the card
- Donte Johnson — 9-0 with seven knockouts and frightening power
- Dustin Stoltzfus — massive upset over a hyped prospect
- Neil Magny — 25 UFC wins and three straight finishes at 39

**Biggest Questions:**
- Who will the UFC give Islam Makhachev next? A Machado Garry rematch?
- How many fights in a row can Islam Makhachev extend the record to?
- Does the UFC give Jeremiah Wells the top 15 fight he asked for, or does he walk?
- Who is Mackenzie Dern's next title defense against?
- How long does Neil Magny keep this up, and can he actually catch Jim Miller?

**Overall Card Score: 8.2**

The main event was historic but not thrilling, and the co-main was a shutout that the crowd sat on its hands for. Outside of the two title fights, the matchups were underwhelming. Eight finishes in twelve fights, a submission that's only happened one other time, and two men laying their gloves down one final time. Most of all, a record that had stood since Anderson Silva was champion was broken after 13 years. Islam Makhachev wants to fight again in the next 5 months and is looking to push that record one win further.
